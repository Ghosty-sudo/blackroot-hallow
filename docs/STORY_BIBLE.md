# Blackroot Hollow — Story Bible v0.1

## Narrative promise
Blackroot Hollow is a compact dark-fantasy frontier story about a town surviving by feeding on the same living power that is consuming it. The player begins believing the Hollow is an infestation beneath Warden's Rest. Repeated descents reveal that the town, its Wardens, and the Heartwood are parts of one old bargain.

The story should deepen the action loop rather than interrupt it. A player who only wants combat can understand the objective in seconds. A player who pays attention should reinterpret the same mechanics — Rootmarks, Root Amber, guardians, death, upgrades, and repeated descents — as the plot unfolds.

## The old bargain
Warden's Rest was founded above an ancient living root network called the Blackroot. Early settlers were dying from blight, winter, and poisoned ground. The first Wardens found the Heartwood: a vast living knot capable of moving vitality through the root network.

They made a bargain. The Heartwood would keep the frontier soil fertile and lend strength to chosen Wardens. In return, the town would return a measured share of life and memory through ritual Rootmarks.

Generations later, the ritual became extraction. Warden's Rest learned to refine Root Amber, store it, and use it for permanent strength. The town forgot that Amber was not ore. It was condensed vitality moving through a living system.

The Blackroot did not suddenly become evil. It began defending itself.

## The player
The player is the newest active Warden after the previous descent teams stopped returning. Their name and detailed past stay deliberately light so the player can inhabit the role.

At first their duty is simple: descend, destroy corrupted guardians, recover Root Amber, strengthen the Rest.

Their unique danger is compatibility. Rootmarks bind to them unusually well. This explains why they can repeatedly accept bargains, carry relics, die or return, and continue pushing deeper. It also makes them valuable to both the town and the Heartwood.

The central character question is not 'Can the Warden kill the monster?' It is 'When the Warden learns what the town is doing, what does duty mean?'

## Warden's Rest
A small fortified settlement built around an old lift/well descending into the Blackroot. The Rest is not secretly populated by villains. Most residents genuinely believe Amber keeps them alive. The moral pressure comes from dependence, inherited lies, and fear rather than cartoon corruption.

## Rootmarks
Rootmarks are fragments of the original bargain. Each gives power by moving a cost somewhere else. Their mechanical tradeoffs are literal story truth.

- Briar Oath: violence answered by violence. The Warden hits harder because the bond opens them further to pain.
- Fleet Root: the root strips durability into motion.
- Blood Sap: stolen vitality returns through the weapon, but the bond thins the Warden's immediate life reserve for that descent.

Future Rootmarks should follow the same rule: the curse explains where the blessing came from.

## Root Amber
Root Amber is hardened life-memory shed by the Blackroot under stress. Banking Amber at Warden's Rest makes upgrades possible, but repeated extraction weakens the Heartwood and intensifies its defensive response.

This turns the existing progression mechanic into narrative pressure without punishing the player for using the upgrade system.

## The three current depths

### I — Gloamgrove
The youngest defensive layer: wet roots, thorn growth, old Warden paths being swallowed by living wood. The player is still told this is simple corruption.

Guardian: **Briar Warden**.

The Briar Warden was once a human Warden who bound too deeply during an earlier emergency. The Blackroot preserved the body as a defensive pattern. Its line charge is the last recognizable echo of a Warden's committed advance.

Story reveal: the monster carries a Warden insignia beneath the briars.

### II — Marrowroot
An older layer where roots grew through burial chambers and absorbed generations of remains and memory. Bone shapes are not an undead army; they are the root network reconstructing forms from what it remembers.

Guardian: **Marrow Bell**.

The Bell formed around the old warning bell lowered underground after the first settlement fire. Its pulse is a distress signal turned into a weapon.

Story reveal: markings around the chamber describe the Heartwood as a covenant, not a mine.

### III — Embermold
The scar left by an old attempt to burn the Blackroot out. Heat never fully left the root system. Fungal growth and charred sap keep the wound alive.

Guardian: **Ember Stag**.

The Stag is a root-memory of frontier wildlife killed in the burn. It attacks in chained dashes because it is eternally replaying flight from the fire.

Story reveal: the fire was ordered by Warden's Rest after the Heartwood resisted increased Amber extraction.

## The fourth boss — Heartwood Sentinel
The current three-guardian clear should stop being the true ending. Defeating Ember Stag opens the final Heartwood chamber.

The **Heartwood Sentinel** is an ancient Warden-shaped organism grown around the first covenant seal. Unlike the corrupted guardians, it speaks clearly in short text because it carries preserved human memory.

Its role is to force the truth into the open before the ending. It does not claim the Blackroot is innocent. It claims the bargain was broken by both fear and hunger.

Mechanical identity target: a readable multi-phase duel that remixes lessons from the three guardians without simply copying them — lane commitment, expanding safe-space pressure, and chained movement — followed by one unique Heartwood mechanic.

## Main ending — The Measured Cut
After defeating the Sentinel, the Warden reaches the Heartwood and refuses both extremes: neither destroy the Heartwood nor let it consume Warden's Rest. The Warden cuts the extraction channels and restores a smaller version of the original covenant. The town loses easy Amber abundance but survives. The Blackroot retreats from active defense.

This resolves the immediate story while leaving room for challenge runs and post-launch material.

## Optional challenge ending — Take the Root
If inexpensive enough after the main release is stable, repeated clears/high progression can unlock a second choice: bind directly to the Heartwood and become the new living Warden of the network. Optional scope, not required for launch.

## Recurring cast targets
Keep the cast tiny. Four recurring voices are enough for release scope.

### Mara Venn — acting Keeper
Runs the descent platform and gives the player their initial duty. Practical, protective, and deeply invested in keeping Warden's Rest alive. She knows the town extracts from the Heartwood but does not initially know the full history.

### Old Fen — former Warden
Survived an earlier descent and refuses another Rootmark. Initially sounds cowardly; later becomes the first person willing to say the guardians are remembering something.

### Tallow — Amber smith
Handles permanent upgrades. Loves the material because it works miracles, then becomes increasingly disturbed when worked Amber begins reacting to the player's returns.

### The Rootvoice
Not a conventional NPC. Short phrases appear at thresholds and after guardian deaths. At first they can be mistaken for atmospheric text. Later it becomes clear the Heartwood is attempting communication.

## Narrative progression across runs
Story progression keys off milestones, not random dialogue spam.

1. First descent: Mara explains duty; Rootmarks are framed as dangerous tools.
2. First Briar Warden kill: insignia reveal; Fen recognizes it.
3. First Marrow Bell kill: covenant language discovered.
4. First Ember Stag kill: old burn order revealed; current victory becomes an opening into Heartwood rather than final resolution.
5. First Heartwood entry: Rootvoice becomes coherent.
6. First Sentinel defeat: main ending.
7. Subsequent clears: short aftermath variants and optional challenge hooks.

Deaths can add flavor lines, but required story information must never depend on dying.

## Dialogue style
- Frontier directness, not faux-Shakespeare.
- Short sentences. Strong nouns. Minimal lore jargon.
- Characters rarely explain what both people already know.
- Humor is dry and human, used to keep the game from becoming grimdark.
- Boss/root lines are strange but understandable.

Example tone:

Mara: "Bring back Amber. Bring yourself back first."

Fen, after Briar Warden: "That badge wasn't grown there. I knew the man who wore it."

Tallow: "Amber never used to pulse on the anvil. Tell me yours didn't."

Rootvoice: "YOU CALL RETURN WHAT WE CALL TAKING."

Sentinel: "The bargain was not broken in one night. Neither will it be mended in one."

## Release-scope rules
- Story must fit the compact 2–4 hour first-ending target.
- No sprawling quest log.
- No romance system.
- No voiced dialogue requirement.
- No collectible-lore checklist needed for comprehension.
- Required exposition appears naturally on the critical path.
- Every new boss/biome should advance both mechanics and the central mystery.
